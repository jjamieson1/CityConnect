# PIPEDA (Canada federal)

## Scope

Canada's federal private-sector privacy law: applies to personal information collected, used or disclosed in the course of commercial activity, and to interprovincial and international flows. Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec have laws deemed substantially similar, so intra-provincial activity there is governed provincially — which is why a Canadian project often carries PIPEDA plus a provincial statute rather than one of them. Reform has been attempted and stalled; the current law is the one to build to.

## What it demands

Ten fair information principles in Schedule 1, of which the operative ones for a build are:

- **Accountability.** A named individual responsible for privacy compliance.
- **Identifying purposes** before or at collection, and **meaningful consent** — the OPC's guidance sets out what meaningful means: emphasise what matters, present options clearly, and use express consent for sensitive information.
- **Limiting collection, use, disclosure and retention.** Collect only what the identified purpose needs; keep it only as long as needed.
- **Accuracy** and **safeguards** proportionate to sensitivity.
- **Openness** and **individual access** — respond to an access request generally within 30 days, and correct on request.
- **Challenging compliance** — a real complaint path.

**Breach of security safeguards** reporting: where a breach creates a **real risk of significant harm**, report to the Privacy Commissioner and notify affected individuals as soon as feasible, notify other organizations that can mitigate, and keep a record of **every** breach — reportable or not — for 24 months.

## How it lands in this app

Consent needs to be recorded, versioned and revocable, with the purpose text as shown at the time. Access requests need an export path. The 24-month record of all breaches means an internal incident register with a harm assessment per incident, including the ones you decide not to report — that decision is the thing that must be defensible later. Cross-border processing is permitted but requires comparable protection by contract and transparency about it.

## Evidence to keep

The privacy policy with purposes, consent records, the incident register with RROSH assessments, access request logs, the accountable individual's designation, and vendor agreements covering transfers.
